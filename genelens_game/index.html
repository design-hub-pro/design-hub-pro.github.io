What the player actually does

Each case starts with one variant. The player reads the changed base, carries it through transcription as a codon puzzle, watches the swapped amino acid land in the folded structure, and then argues for what it does to the protein. The game scores the chain of reasoning, not the final answer.

The AI companion sits on a three-position slider. The same variant can be explained to a ten-year-old, a biology student, or someone who already knows what a hydrophobic core is. Changing the level rewrites the explanation, not the model on screen.

Because the slider is a setting rather than a cutscene, it doubles as the study instrument. A research build can log which level a player chooses, how long they stay there, and whether their later answers improve.

Scope notes and one thing we would cut

Cut the disease-prediction claim. The brief asks the game to show how a mutation "contributes to disease". A student build cannot do that honestly. Predicting pathogenicity is an open research problem, and a confident wrong answer in a teaching tool is worse than no answer. The game should stop at structural consequence: this residue sits in the core, the substitution is larger and charged, the local packing breaks. Known clinical interpretations can be shown as cited database entries, labelled as other people's conclusions.

Structures come from real data. Protein geometry should be loaded from the PDB or AlphaFold DB rather than modelled by hand, so the highlighted residue is in the position the data says it is. Variant records would come from ClinVar. No structure, no case.

Art is concept, not final. All ten frames here are AI-generated concept art for look and layout. They are not screenshots of a running build, and the on-screen text in them is illustrative.

Ten cases, not a genome. The chromosome map suggests a full genome. A first build should ship ten hand-checked cases across three or four well-studied genes and let the map show the rest as locked. Breadth here is cheap to fake and expensive to get right.
