Research Plan

Food Chemistry First, AI-Assisted

The research centers on food chemistry — a substitution rule database grounded in chemical mechanisms, validated through real kitchen experiments, and written up as a research paper. AI plays a supporting role: applying the rules to recipes at scale.

Deliverable 1

Substitution Rule Database

50–100 ingredient substitution rules, each documented with: original → substitute, the chemical mechanism, target condition (diabetes / hypertension / dental), quantitative effect, and data source.

Example: Sucrose → allulose. Allulose participates in the Maillard reaction (browning preserved) but contributes ~0.4 kcal/g and a near-zero glycemic response — unlike erythritol, which doesn't brown.

Example: NaCl → KCl + mushroom powder. Potassium chloride cuts sodium ~50%; glutamate-driven umami masks KCl's metallic bitterness.

Deliverable 2

10–15 Recipe Transformation Experiments

Cook the original and modified version of each recipe side by side, then measure and compare:

  • Nutrition deltas (sodium, sugar, sat. fat, fiber) computed from USDA FoodData Central
  • Estimated glycemic load shift using the Sydney GI database
  • Browning, rise, and texture observations tied to the predicted chemistry
  • Blind taste panel scored on the 9-point hedonic scale (Peryam & Pilgrim, 1957)

Deliverable 3

Research Paper

A full write-up in standard research-paper format: introduction & related work, database construction methodology, experiment protocol, quantitative results, sensory evaluation, and discussion of which chemical mechanisms survived real-kitchen validation.

Target: submission to the Journal of Emerging Investigators (peer-reviewed journal for pre-college research) and use in science fair / college applications.

Candidate Experiment Recipes

Selected from the Food.com dataset (180K+ recipes with reviews) for popularity among older adults and coverage of distinct chemical mechanisms. Final 10–15 chosen so each major rule category is tested at least twice.

Baked Goods — sugar & fat chemistry

Chicken noodle soup · Congee with century egg · Miso soup

Desserts — glycemic load & gelation

Rice pudding · Egg tart · Red bean soup (低糖红豆沙)

Datasets & References

Every rule in the database cites at least one of these sources; every experiment's nutrition numbers are computed from them.

Resource What It Provides How We Use It
Food Chemistry & Composition
USDA FoodData Central Lab-analyzed nutrient profiles for 300K+ foods, free public API Before/after nutrition deltas for every rule and every experiment
FooDB 70K+ chemical compounds found in foods, with concentrations The chemical mechanism documented behind each substitution rule
FlavorDB2 (IIIT-Delhi) 25K flavor molecules and which ingredients share them Choose substitutes that preserve the flavor-molecule overlap of the original
Sydney University GI Database Measured glycemic index values for ~5,000 foods Diabetes-targeted rules; estimated glycemic-load shift per experiment
IDDSI Framework International standard texture levels for dysphagia & dental issues Texture-modification rules for chewing/swallowing difficulty
Recipe Datasets
Food.com Recipes & Interactions (Kaggle) 180K+ recipes with structured ingredients plus 700K user reviews Source of the 10–15 experiment recipes; reviews contain real-world substitution reports to validate rules against
RecipeNLG 2.2M recipes with normalized ingredient lists Measure how many real recipes each rule applies to (coverage statistics for the paper)
Recipe1M+ (MIT CSAIL) 1M recipes paired with food images Optional AI component: recipe parsing and the fridge-scan ingredient detector
Prior Research & Methodology
