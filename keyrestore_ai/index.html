The Problem

Motor Decline Robs Seniors of Independence

Over 10 million people worldwide live with Parkinson's disease, experiencing progressive loss of fine motor control. Combined with 55 million dementia patients globally, millions of seniors face shrinking independence as their hands lose dexterity. Traditional rehabilitation is costly, repetitive, and often inaccessible — leaving most patients without engaging tools to fight back.

Social Impact

Healing Hands Through Music

Motor Skill Recovery

Piano-based therapy improves finger dexterity and hand coordination in Parkinson's patients. Studies show structured keyboard exercises significantly enhance fine motor control and reduce tremor severity.

PMC Research

Cognitive Stimulation

Music therapy activates multiple brain regions simultaneously — memory, motor planning, and emotional processing. For dementia patients, familiar melodies can improve verbal fluency and reduce agitation.

Alzheimer's Research & Therapy

At-Home Rehabilitation

Only 30% of Parkinson's patients have access to regular physical therapy. A portable, LED-guided piano pad brings rehabilitation into the home — making daily practice accessible, affordable, and engaging.

Michael J. Fox Foundation

Data-Driven Care

AI-powered progress tracking gives caregivers and physicians objective data on motor function over time. This enables earlier intervention when decline is detected and validates the effectiveness of treatment plans.

Project in Action

Real-world scenarios showing how KeyRestore AI helps seniors regain motor control

LED-Guided Piano Practice

Robert follows the illuminated keys on his KeyRestore Pad, playing "Twinkle Twinkle" at his own pace while the app tracks his accuracy and finger response time.

Caregiver Progress Review

A caregiver reviews weekly dexterity reports, noticing improvements in ring finger strength and sharing the data with Robert's neurologist.

Group Rehabilitation Session

At an assisted living facility, residents use individual KeyRestore Pads to play together, combining motor rehabilitation with social engagement.

Adaptive Difficulty Engine

The AI detects that pinky finger response is lagging and automatically adjusts exercises to focus on that finger, personalizing each session in real time.

Growth Path

From Pilot to Platform

1

Clinical Pilot

Partner with a local neurology clinic or Parkinson's support group. Test the prototype with 10-15 patients, measuring dexterity improvements over 8 weeks. Refine the adaptive difficulty algorithm based on real patient data.

2

Hardware Production

Design a compact, affordable piano pad with pressure sensors and LED-lit keys using Arduino or Raspberry Pi. Partner with university engineering labs for prototyping. Target a production cost under $50 per unit.

3

Healthcare Integration

Build a clinical dashboard for occupational therapists and neurologists. Seek FDA Class I exemption for the device. Partner with insurance providers to explore coverage for at-home rehabilitation tools.

Foundation

Music in the Family

"My grandfather taught me piano when I was seven. His hands were steady then, his melodies filled the house. Music was our language — the way we connected without words."

Observation

Watching the Tremors

"When Parkinson's took hold, I watched his fingers freeze over the keys. The music stopped, but the longing in his eyes never did. I realized that traditional rehab felt like a chore — but music could make it joyful."

Insight

The LED Breakthrough

"What if the piano could guide him? LED lights showing which key to press, at a pace adapted to his tremors? What if each practice session was both therapy and music — measured by AI to track his recovery?"

Innovation

Building KeyRestore AI
