ISEF · Behavioral & Social Sciences

From a product to a
testable behavioral question.

Ballotwise isn't only an app — its vote-simulation feature is a natural experiment. The moment we show a user "people like you lean NDP +5," we are introducing social proof into a decision. ISEF Behavioral Science rewards a sharp, falsifiable question with clean controls and ethics — so we turn that one design choice into a study of the bandwagon effect on undecided voters.

Research Question
Does showing demographic-matched peer preference ("people like you") shift undecided voters' candidate choice and confidence — independent of the candidates' actual platforms?
Hypothesis (H1): Undecided participants shown demographic-matched peer data will switch toward the displayed plurality at a higher rate, and report greater voting confidence, than participants shown candidate information alone — demonstrating informational social influence (bandwagon), not reasoned belief change.
1

Frame & isolate

Narrow the broad product to one mechanism with a clear cause→effect: social-proof display → vote intention.

  • One IV, measurable DVs
  • Grounded in conformity & nudge theory
  • Falsifiable, not "does the app help?"
2

Randomized design

A within-app A/B/C experiment using identical candidate cards; only the social-proof layer changes.

  • A Control — info only
  • B Riding-wide aggregate
  • C Demographic-matched "like you"
3

Measure the shift

Capture pre/post choice and confidence; analyze switching and self-reported reasoning.

  • Pre/post candidate pick
  • Confidence (1–7 Likert)
  • Decision time & debrief reasons

Variables

  • Independent: type of social-proof shown (none / aggregate / demographic-matched)
  • Dependent: preference switch rate, change in confidence, decision time
  • Controlled: identical candidate text, counterbalanced order, fictional riding to remove real-world bias

Analysis & sample

  • n ≈ 150 voluntary participants, random assignment
  • χ² test on switching; one-way ANOVA on confidence change
  • Logistic regression controlling for baseline partisanship & political interest

Human-participant ethics (required for ISEF)

Because this studies real decision behavior, it needs SRC/IRB approval before testing, informed consent, and full anonymity (no PII). A mandatory debrief makes clear the simulation is fictional and never affects real elections — keeping the bandwagon manipulation low-risk and reversible, consistent with the app's own non-partisan, opt-in design.
