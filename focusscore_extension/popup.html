The Quiet Insistence of "Letter from Birmingham Jail"

King opens not with outrage but with courtesy. By addressing the eight clergymen as "men of genuine good will," he grants them the very reasonableness he will later show they lack. This concession is strategic: it places King on the high ground of civility before he says a single word about injustice.

The shift arrives in the long periodic sentence that begins "when you have seen vicious mobs." King stacks clause on clause, each one heavier than the last, and the reader is forced to wait, much as the people he describes were forced to wait. The structure itself becomes the argument
