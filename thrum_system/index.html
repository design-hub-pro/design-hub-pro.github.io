Wearable haptics

Thrum, a vest that lets you feel the shape of a song

Five motors across the back carry five bands of the music. The phone draws the same five bands.

Battery powered, and under 70 ms behind the music.

How it works

The phone does the listening. The vest does the telling.

SensePhone taps its own audio
SplitFFT into five bands
DecideOnset and level per band
SendOne packet every 7.5 ms
ActFive motors at 175 Hz
ShowScreen draws the same bands

The driver is the one step that is not obvious. The actuator is a mass on a spring; left alone it needs about 30 ms to reach full amplitude, turning a drum hit into a swell. The DRV2605L overdrives it for 8 ms and brakes it at the end, which buys the 12 ms below.

How we know it works

Three numbers decide whether this is an instrument or a novelty

The vest has to stay in time with the music, and the five channels have to stay separate on a moving body. Both get measured before either is claimed.

Latency

The phone's line out goes to one scope channel; an accelerometer taped over the centre pod goes to the other. Trigger on the audio transient, read the time until the skin reaches half of steady vibration. One hundred impulses across three tracks, on a rigid torso form and then on three wearers. Accept a median under 70 ms and a 95th percentile under 100 ms, against a design budget of 57 ms.

Separation

Drive one pod at rated amplitude and record acceleration at the other four. A neighbouring site has to read at least 12 dB down. Neoprene conducts vibration well, so this is measured on a person, not on a bench.

Onsets

Beats the firmware fires must land within 25 ms of a reference onset detector run over the same file, on 90% of onsets, across ten tracks in four genres.

If separation fails at five sites, the answer is three sites, not more processing. If latency fails, the radio is the first suspect and a wire is the demonstration fallback. None of this says the vest feels good: six deaf and hard-of-hearing testers rank paired clips at the end, reported apart from the instrument figures.

Changes to the brief

Three things in the brief do not survive contact with the parts

Eight channels down to five

Every actuator resonates at one frequency, near 175 Hz. Eight bands driving eight motors reach the body as eight envelopes at the same pitch, so the only thing separating a bass note from a cymbal is where it lands. Eight also breaks the supply: eight motors plus the radio draw 1001 mA against a 1 A regulator. Five leaves 28% margin, and the motors saved pay for the measurement that decides whether five is itself too many.

The gain belongs to the wearer, not to the code

A fixed map from band energy to drive level cannot work. Thresholds vary by roughly 15 dB between people, a cotton shirt costs another 6 to 10 dB, and a quiet verse can sit 30 dB below a chorus. That is a 50 dB spread against about 20 dB of usable actuator range. A 30 second staircase at wear time finds each person's threshold at each site, and per-band gain with a four second release absorbs the rest.

The parts list has no regulator, and it needs one

A single-cell battery runs from 4.2 V down to 3.2 V. A linear regulator drops out near 3.5 V, at roughly 40% charge remaining, and below that the rail follows the battery. Actuator output goes as about the square of drive voltage, so the vest would quietly weaken as it discharged. A buck-boost holds 3.3 V to the floor, and 470 uF of bulk covers the 475 mA step when five motors start together.

What could go wrong

The soldering is a weekend. The rest is not.

The hard part is making a continuous bass line feel like anything. Skin adapts to steady vibration in about twenty seconds, so a vest driven by band level alone becomes a buzz carrying no information. The firmware has to key on onsets and let go between them. Tuning that is where the weeks go.

The reserve is one wideband actuator on the sternum carrying the whole signal, with the pitch information moved to the screen. That decision gets made at the end of week six, straight after the separation measurement.

Then the dull ones. Contact pressure couples the pod to the body, so a strap that slips a centimetre takes real amplitude with it. Motor leads break at the solder joint after a few hundred flex cycles, hence the service loop at every pod.

For the engineer

Build detail

Working specification. Not needed to understand the project.

Band split, onset detection, gain
  • 44.1 kHz mono sum, 1024-point Hann FFT, hop 512 (11.6 ms), 43 Hz per bin.
  • Bands: 20–120, 120–400, 400–1.2k, 1.2k–4k, 4k–12k Hz. Site order is low at the spine, high at the shoulders.
  • Per band: spectral flux, half-wave rectified, median filter over 7 frames, adaptive threshold at 4× the rolling noise floor.
  • Gain: attack 5 ms, release 4 s, floor at each wearer's calibrated threshold, ceiling at rated amplitude.
  • Refractory period 40 ms per band, which caps a single site at 25 events per second.
Link and firmware state machine
  • BLE 5.0, connection interval 7.5 ms, notify only, no acknowledgement. A dropped packet holds the previous level rather than muting.
  • Packet: 5 bytes of amplitude, 1 byte of onset flags, 1 sequence byte, 1 checksum. 8 bytes total.
  • States: IDLE, CALIBRATE, RUN, BROWNOUT, CHARGE. BROWNOUT is entered below 3.25 V at the cell and drops to two channels rather than shutting off mid-song.
  • Watchdog at 500 ms. No packet for 300 ms brakes every actuator to zero.
Power budget and endurance
  • Peak: 240 mA radio + 25 mA drivers + 450 mA actuators + 1 mA mux = 716 mA against a 1 A regulator.
  • Average on dense material, motors at 50% duty: 330 mA at 3.3 V, about 1.09 W.
  • Cell 3.7 V × 2000 mAh = 7.4 Wh. At 85% converter efficiency that is about 5.8 hours, against a two-hour session.
  • Charge at 500 mA from USB-C, roughly 4.5 hours from empty.
The app

Same five numbers feed the screen and the motors, taken from the same frame, so the two cannot drift apart by more than one frame of the display.

  • Five vertical bars, bottom to top matching spine to shoulders, so the picture is laid out like the body.
  • 60 fps, one frame of buffering, +16 ms behind the haptics. Acceptance is ±25 ms between the bar peak and the pod.
  • Calibration screen: per-site staircase, three reversals, about 30 seconds.
  • Session log as CSV, one row per onset with band, level, and timestamp. This is what the validation runs read.
Bring-up order

Every part is tested alone before any two are joined.

  • Regulator on a bench supply, sweep 4.2 V down to 3.0 V, confirm 3.3 V holds and measure the sag under a 700 mA pulsed load.
  • One driver, one actuator, on the bench, no vest. Confirm overdrive and braking on a scope.
  • Mux with two drivers, then five, checking for address conflicts.
  • Radio link alone, logging round-trip time for an hour.
  • One pod moulded and worn before five are made. The first pod is always wrong.
Validation checklist
  • DAC loopback delay measured and recorded before any latency capture.
  • Latency: 100 impulses, 3 tracks, torso form then 3 wearers. Median < 70 ms, 95th < 100 ms.
  • Separation: each of 5 sites driven alone, 4 neighbours read, 3 wearers, 3 repeats. Neighbour ≥ 12 dB down.
  • Onsets: 10 tracks, 4 genres, ≥ 90% within 25 ms of the reference detector.
  • Screen against pod: 200 frames, ±25 ms.
  • Endurance: full discharge on dense material, logged. Report the number, do not round it up.
  • Amplitude against battery state: same track at 4.2 V and at 3.3 V, acceleration within 10%.
  • Preference: 6 deaf and hard-of-hearing testers, paired comparison, reported separately.
What this device cannot do
  • It does not convey pitch. Every actuator runs at 175 Hz; frequency is coded as position, and position is coarse.
  • It does not convey timbre, harmony, or words. A piano and a guitar playing the same line feel close to identical.
  • It is not a hearing aid or a substitute for one, and it makes no clinical claim.
  • It cannot follow a live room without a feed. Microphone mode adds the room's reverberation and roughly 20 ms.
  • Five channels is an upper bound set by the crosstalk measurement, not a target. Three is a plausible outcome.
  • Two smaller corrections: the drivers share one fixed I2C address and need the mux, and the accelerometer is validation hardware, not part of the product.

Assumed, and worth correcting if wrong: a phone that can expose its own playback buffer, one builder, and a bench with a two-channel oscilloscope.
