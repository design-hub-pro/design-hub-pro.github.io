The Problem

Sound is a Critical Safety Signal Most Take for Granted

For the 466 million people worldwide with disabling hearing loss, everyday sounds that signal danger—fire alarms, gunshots, car horns, and sirens—go unnoticed. This creates a constant state of vulnerability where life-threatening situations can escalate without warning, particularly in public spaces, at night, or during emergencies.

Fire alarms rely entirely on auditory signals that deaf individuals cannot perceive

Emergency situations require immediate awareness that sound-dependent alerts cannot provide

Project in Action

Real-world scenarios showing how AlertBand AI keeps users safe

Safe Sleep Mode

AlertBand's enhanced vibration intensity during sleep hours ensures users wake up to fire alarms, smoke detectors, or intruder alerts.

Workplace Integration

Discreet alerts during meetings and work activities keep deaf employees informed of fire drills, emergency announcements, and building alarms.

Urban Navigation

Detects approaching emergency vehicles, car horns, and crossing signals to enhance pedestrian safety in busy city environments.

Active Lifestyle

Water-resistant design with adjustable sensitivity allows users to stay alert during workouts, running, or outdoor activities.

Growth Path

From Prototype to Product

1

Build & Test Prototype

Assemble hardware with Arduino/ESP32, train initial ML model on sound datasets (ESC-50, AudioSet), and test with local deaf community members.

2

Partner with Organizations

Collaborate with schools for the deaf, Gallaudet University, and organizations like NAD to gather feedback and expand testing to real-world environments.

3

Launch & Scale

Apply for accessibility grants, seek partnerships with hearing aid manufacturers, and work toward FDA/CE certification for medical wearables.
