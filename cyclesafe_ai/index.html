The Problem

Cyclists Face Critical Blind Spots in Traffic

Every year, thousands of cyclists are injured or killed in traffic accidents, with rear-end collisions and side-swipes being among the most common causes. Traditional mirrors offer limited visibility, and bad weather conditions like rain, fog, and low light dramatically reduce a driver's ability to see cyclists, making rides increasingly dangerous.

Project in Action

Real-world scenarios showing how CycleSafe AI keeps cyclists safe

Urban Commute Protection

AI radar continuously scans for vehicles approaching from behind, alerting commuters through vibration and sound before danger approaches.

Rainy Day Safety Mode

Weather sensors automatically increase alert sensitivity and LED brightness when rain is detected, compensating for reduced driver visibility.

Night Riding Confidence

Automatic low-light detection activates enhanced LED patterns and extends radar range, making evening commutes safer.

Close Pass Detection

Camera system measures passing distance and logs dangerous close passes, providing data for safety advocacy and reporting.

Growth Path

From Prototype to Product

1

Build & Test Prototype

Assemble the sensor array using Arduino or Raspberry Pi, integrate camera module, and develop basic object detection algorithms. Test with local cycling groups.

2

Campus Pilot Program

Partner with university bike-share programs for real-world testing. Collect data on detection accuracy, false positives, and user feedback to iterate on the design.

3

Scale with Partners

Connect with cycling advocacy groups and urban transportation departments. Seek grants for cycling safety technology and explore B2B partnerships with bike manufacturers.

Community Initiative

SafeWheels Initiative

A student-led non-profit organization dedicated to making cycling safer and more accessible for everyone in our community.

Our Mission

Beyond technology, building a culture of cycling safety

To eliminate preventable cycling injuries in our community through education, accessibility, and advocacy—ensuring that every person, regardless of income or background, has the knowledge and resources to ride safely.

What We Do

Visibility Kit Drives

Collect and distribute free safety kits containing reflective stickers, spoke lights, and ankle bands to cyclists who need them most—especially delivery workers and students.

Monthly distribution events

School Safety Workshops

Partner with middle and high schools to deliver engaging 30-minute workshops on cycling safety, hand signals, helmet fitting, and navigating traffic confidently.

Peer-to-peer education model

Safe Route Mapping

Crowdsource and publish an interactive map of the safest cycling routes in our city, highlighting protected bike lanes, well-lit paths, and dangerous intersections to avoid.

Community-sourced data
