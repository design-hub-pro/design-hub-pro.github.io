Black-throated Blue Warbler

Setophaga caerulescens

Least concern Migratory Spring visitor

Small warbler with strong sexual dimorphism. Males show slate-blue back, white belly, and black face/throat; females are olive-brown above with pale lemon-yellow underparts.

Length
11–13 cm
Weight
8.4–12 g
Diet
Insects, spiders, berries
Lifespan
~5 years (wild)

How to identify

  • 1
    Slate-blue upper-parts on adult male — striking and unmistakable in good light.
  • 2
    White "handkerchief" wing patch on both sexes — a key field mark.
  • 3
    Wheezy rising buzz: "zur-zur-zur-ZREE" — a distinctive ascending song.
  • 4
    Mid-canopy forager — gleans insects from underside of leaves.
Confusables

Female may be mistaken for Tennessee Warbler — check for the white wing patch.

Range & habitat

Eastern North America
Breeding Migration

Breeds in mature deciduous and mixed forests with dense understory; overwinters in the Caribbean. Travis County is at the western edge of its migration route — most local sightings occur in late April and May.
