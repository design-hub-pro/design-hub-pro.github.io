The Problem

Why We Need Better Public Health Education

During disease outbreaks, public understanding often diverges from scientific reality. People assume "higher fatality always means more dangerous" when in fact a highly transmissible virus with low fatality can cause more total deaths. Traditional education fails to convey these counterintuitive dynamics that are critical for informed policy support and personal decisions.

Pandemic simulation games show significant improvement in situational awareness and public health knowledge among students, with reduced anxiety and increased confidence in understanding health emergencies.

Your Journey

From Curiosity to Creation

This project connects your interest in science, gaming, and making a difference in public understanding.

The Narrative Arc: From Questions to Answers

Foundation: A Puzzling Question

"During the pandemic, I noticed people making decisions that seemed illogical—like fearing one disease while ignoring a more dangerous one. I wanted to understand why."

Observation: The Math vs Intuition Gap

"I learned that R₀ and case fatality rate work together in complex ways. A virus with high R₀ but low fatality can kill more people than the reverse—but our brains don't naturally see this."

Insight: Learning Through Experience

"Reading about epidemiology didn't change my intuition—but when I played simulation games, I started to 'feel' how transmission rates compound. I realized games could teach what textbooks couldn't."

Innovation: EpiSim

"I built EpiSim to help others develop the same intuition—not by telling them facts, but by letting them experience the consequences of epidemic dynamics firsthand."
