Why I picked this video

Watch from 2:10 to 6:30. Before that it covers stroke order, which we do in Lesson 3. The part that matters is where he groups characters by radical instead of by lesson number. Ignore his claim that radicals always predict meaning. They usually hint, and roughly one in five will mislead you.

The idea in one line

Most characters are built from two parts. One part hints at the meaning, the other hints at the sound. Chinese teachers call these 形声字, phono-semantic compounds, and they are somewhere around 80% of the characters you will meet in an SL text.

Where this breaks

Radicals are a hint, not a rule. 海 has the water radical and means sea, which is fair. But 洗 (to wash) and 汤 (soup) both use it too, and you would never guess "soup" from "water" alone. Use the radical to narrow the field, then confirm with context. Do not treat it as a translation.

Quick check

Four characters you have probably not been taught. Guess the topic from the radical alone.

Question 1 of 4 No score is recorded for this one.
怒
The bottom component is 心. What kind of word is this most likely to be?
怒 nù · anger

心 sits at the bottom rather than the left, which is normal for feelings that are states rather than actions. Compare 想 (to think), 忘 (to forget), 急 (anxious, urgent). The top part 奴 carries the sound.
