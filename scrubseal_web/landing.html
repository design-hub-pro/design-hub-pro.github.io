Zero cameras. Zero microphones recording speech.

A badge tap only counts when the hands were actually washed.

ScrubSeal clips under the sink. It checks water flow, a soap pump press and a 20-second scrub. If all three happen, the badge tap is sealed into the log. If not, the supervisor sees a badge-only tap.

Most hand hygiene audits are a person with a clipboard. Or a camera.

Clipboards miss nights and weekends. Cameras make volunteers feel watched, and many clinics cannot put them near patients. ScrubSeal reads the sink, not the person.

Three signals, one seal

A pipe vibration sensor hears water running. A tilt switch on the soap pump counts presses. A short-range motion sensor checks the scrub rhythm lasts 20 seconds. Only then does the badge tap get written.

Tamper-evident

Each record carries the hash of the one before it.

Pseudonymous

Devices store badge hashes, never names.

PPE by role

Optional glove and mask tags, rules set per role.

What the supervisor sees

Missed washes and badge-only taps, by shift and by station.

Not a leaderboard. A short list of what needs a conversation today, and a monthly report that an inspector can verify byte for byte.
