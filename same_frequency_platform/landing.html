Does real understanding need more contact — or better-matched contact?

Same Frequency studies why some people feel closest when they talk often, and others feel closest sitting in silence. It doesn't start from "old people are lonely, so young people should visit more." It starts from a sharper question: do different people need to be understood in different ways?

Enter the research console See the current findings

Not a diagnosis project

The study looks for different communication styles — quiet, slow-response, structure-seeking, stimulus-sensitive — never for people to label. Cognitive or medical background is only recorded if a participant offers it, unprompted.

A personal question, tested rigorously

Cynthia values boundaries and distance, and doesn't assume closeness or frequent contact equals understanding. The project turns that instinct into something interviewable, measurable, and testable — not just asserted.

Design, not just a report

The output isn't only a paper. It's a toolkit of low-pressure formats — cards, music, objects, quiet shared activity — so no one is forced to connect through open chat alone.

Ethical boundaries, held from day one

This is exempt student research, not a clinical study. Every rule below exists so a participant's dignity comes before a data point.

  • Silence, slow response, or repetition is never read as neurodivergence.
  • No participant is asked for a diagnosis; medical history is never required.
  • Every question, and the study itself, can be skipped or exited at any time.
  • Nothing is recorded or published without explicit, separate consent.
  • Consent for a participant with reduced capacity runs through family or a facility, never Cynthia's own judgment.
  • The project does not claim to treat or improve autism, dementia, or any condition.
