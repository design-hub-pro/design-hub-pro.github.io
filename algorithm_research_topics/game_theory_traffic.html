Basic Background

Selfish routing, equilibrium & the Price of Anarchy

Traffic congestion is not only a shortest-path problem. It is also a multi-agent decision-making problem. Every driver wants to choose the fastest route for themselves. However, if too many drivers choose the same "best" route, that route becomes crowded and may become slower for everyone.

This creates a classic game-theory situation called selfish routing. Each driver acts rationally from their own perspective, but the overall system may become inefficient. In traffic theory, this is often studied using Wardrop equilibrium, where no driver can improve their travel time by switching routes alone. However, this equilibrium may not be socially optimal.

The gap between selfish behavior and system-wide optimal routing is often measured by the Price of Anarchy. It asks: how much worse can the total traffic outcome become when every driver optimizes only for themselves?

Brief Research Plan

Five-stage simulation study

From baseline shortest-path routing to game-theoretic coordination and network-scale experiments.

1
Build a traffic network simulation
  • Model roads as a graph.
  • Nodes represent intersections.
  • Edges represent roads with travel-time functions.
  • Travel time increases as more cars use the same road.
2
Implement baseline routing
  • Selfish shortest-path routing: every driver chooses the fastest route for themselves.
  • System-optimal routing: assign drivers to minimize total travel time.
3
Implement game-theoretic routing
  • Simulate drivers repeatedly choosing routes.
  • Allow route choices to converge toward equilibrium.
  • Add optional incentives, such as rewards for choosing less congested roads.
4
Compare routing strategies
  • Average travel time.
  • Total system travel time.
  • Congestion on each road.
  • Fairness among drivers.
  • Stability of route choices.
5
Extend the study
  • Test simple networks first, such as two parallel roads.
  • Then test a larger city-style network.
  • Study how results change as the number of drivers increases.
