Story 1 · Headline issue

"Airbnb stole my block"

3,556 East Side residents this week made the same specific claim: short-term rentals are taking units off the market on a handful of blocks — Brookline & 9th, Pine Row, and the southern half of Oak. The story is hyper-local. The fix can be too: a per-host cap and registration pulled directly from residents' own asks.

"There are 7 Airbnbs on my block. SEVEN. And I'm priced out." That post on r/EastsideWBK earned 1,240 upvotes and was cross-posted into 14 community Slacks within 36 hours.

What residents asked for
  1. 1.Cap STR units per host at 1
  2. 2.Public STR registry by address
  3. 3.Tip line for unregistered hosts

Story 2 · Where the city is winning

"Do this in every district."

Maple Lofts is your only major positive cluster this week, and it's working because it's concrete. Three things distinguish it in resident language: it's named (residents say "Maple Lofts" not "the project"), it's measurable (40% under-market), and it's pedestrian (residents mention walking to it).

"Maple Lofts is what we asked for. Do this in every district."

Story 3 · The signal you'll see by Wednesday

A 412% volume spike on Cedar Park — and it's not about housing.

An 18-hour spike in Cedar Park posts is tracking to a late-night music venue on 4th Street that residents say is breaking permit hours. The conversation is small but doubling every 6 hours. Communications recommends a holding statement by Wednesday before the local paper picks it up — currently 92% of posts skew negative, 71% mention noise, 18% mention parking.

It is the kind of story that becomes a column unless a council member responds to it first.
