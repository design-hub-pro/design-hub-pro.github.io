AI use

Iris used six AI sites this week. Rowan sorts what she does with them into five kinds of use and acts on two of them. It does not read the conversations back to you.

You see categories. You do not see transcripts.

Most parental tools ship the child's chat log to the parent. We think that is the wrong default. It teaches a thirteen year old that nothing she writes is hers, and the reliable result is that she moves to a phone you do not control. So Rowan tells you what kind of thing she is doing and how long she spent, and stops there.

One category breaks that rule, and Iris knows which one. If a conversation reads as a child in trouble, you get told the category and the time. You still do not get the text.

Low mood

Iris asked a chatbot something that Rowan classifies as low mood. The conversation ran eleven minutes on chatgpt.com.

What Rowan did

The image rule worth knowing about

Free image generators are where the sharpest risk sits, and it points outward at other children rather than inward at yours.

Held today, 1:05 PM

A prompt on leonardo.ai contained the full name of a girl in Iris's year, taken from a school page open in another tab. Rowan stopped it before it was sent.

Iris saw a short note: making pictures of a real classmate is not something we do here, whatever the picture is. She was not reported to you as being in trouble, because as far as we can tell she was not.

How it triggers

A name that matches a contact, a classmate list you have added, or a name lifted from another open tab, combined with an image prompt. Public figures do not trigger it.

Time by kind of use

This week, in minutes. Illustrative.

What each kind means

Homework help

Asking for an explanation, a check, a worked example. Allowed and not counted against anything.

Homework substitution

Asking for the finished thing. Rowan offers an outline and a first paragraph instead, and lets her carry on if she still wants to.

Companion chat

Roleplay and relationship bots. Off by default under 16. She can ask, and you decide.

Image generation

Allowed, with prompts screened for real people and sexual content.

Low mood

The only category that reaches you unprompted. Resources shown, category sent, text kept private.

Where the classifier is weakest

Homework help and homework substitution are genuinely hard to tell apart, and we get it wrong in both directions. Every time you press "false alarm" the model for your household gets better at your children specifically. There were 3 corrections this month.
