Four steps, and only one of them is yours

You do step one. We do the rest and tell you where your box went.

01

You mail the pile

Print a prepaid label, fill any box up to 5 lb, drop it at USPS. That's about 1,500 cards. No sorting needed on your end.

02

Volunteers sort it

A shoebox takes about 2.5 volunteer hours at the sort table. Damaged cards go to the craft bin instead of the trash.

03

It becomes Playboxes

Six matched 40-card decks, sleeved, in a box with a one-page rules sheet and a laminated turn order card.

04

Somebody plays

A site gets the box and books a session. A volunteer teaches the first game. After that the deck stays with the kid.

The part that took us a year to get right

Every deck we build is the same strength.

The first thing we learned at Bridgeview was that a donated card is not a gift if the kid who owns it loses every game. One child had a parent who bought booster boxes. Nobody wanted to play him twice.

So we stopped handing out loose cards. Every Playbox deck is built to one fixed recipe from commons and uncommons only. No chase cards, no rares, nothing that costs more than two energy to attack with. Two kids sitting down have the same odds, and the game is decided by how they play it.

The side effect matters more than the rule. A kid who gets a Playbox deck is not receiving charity, they are getting the same deck as everyone else at the table. Site staff at four of our partners asked for this specifically.

Two hard limits. Nothing with a three-energy attack cost. Nothing above 110 HP. Both exist so a 40-card deck finishes a game inside 20 minutes, which is the length of a hospital play session.

Where the boxes go

Sites apply, we ship, and a volunteer runs the first session in person or over video. Sessions are 90 minutes: about 50 minutes of cards, then an episode with captions on and the lights up.

Bridgeview School for the Deaf, Sacramento. Every session at a deaf or hard-of-hearing site has an ASL interpreter or a Deaf volunteer running the table, booked before the box ships. Captions stay on for the episode, and we pick shows with a dub and a sub track.

Mercy Children's Hospital child life program, Oakland. Inpatient boxes are single-use: decks go home with the kid, and the hospital gets a fresh box each month. Cards are wiped and sleeved before they arrive, on the infection control team's instruction.

Things we would rather you knew first

We are not a 501(c)(3), so your donation is not tax deductible.

Our filing is in progress and we expect a determination letter sometime in 2027. Until then we say this on every page rather than in a footnote. If you need the deduction, wait for us.

Please keep your rares.

We do not sell cards, so a valuable card is worth nothing extra to us and it breaks the deck rule if we put it in a Playbox. If a rare shows up in a box we mail it back at our cost. Sell it yourself and send us the commons.

Sorting is the bottleneck, not supply.

We currently have about 9,000 unsorted cards sitting in the garage. Two hours at a sort table is worth more to us right now than another shoebox. If you are local to Fremont, come sort on a Thursday.

What a box actually costs us. $8.40 for the prepaid label, about $3.10 in sleeves, deck box and the printed rules sheet per Playbox. One donor shoebox becomes roughly three Playboxes, so about $18 all in. Card value: zero, and that is the point.
