Social Impact

Why This Matters Now

Fashion's Carbon Footprint

The fashion industry is responsible for 10% of global carbon emissions — more than all international flights and maritime shipping combined.

Earth.org

Massive Water Consumption

Fashion consumes 141 billion cubic meters of water annually. A single t-shirt requires 2,700 liters of water — enough for one person to drink for 900 days.

Economics Observatory

Secondhand Market Explosion

The secondhand apparel market reached $196 billion in 2023 and is projected to hit $593 billion by 2031, with 58% of US shoppers buying secondhand in 2024.

Capital One Research

Donation Makes a Difference

Each secondhand clothing purchase displaces 8 kg of CO2 and reduces emissions by 82% compared to buying new. Goodwill alone receives 6 billion pounds of donations yearly.

Growth Path

From 1 to 100

1

Launch at Your School's Environmental Club

Partner with your school's eco club or sustainability committee to run a pilot program. Organize a "Digital Wardrobe Challenge" where students digitize their closets and track how many new outfit combinations they can create without buying new clothes. Collect feedback and testimonials from early users.

2

Partner with Local Goodwill or Salvation Army

Reach out to your local donation centers to integrate them as donation partners in the app. Propose a co-branded clothing drive where your app helps users identify items to donate, and the charity handles pickup. This creates a win-win that helps both your user acquisition and their donation volume.

3

Present at Earth Day Community Event

Apply to present EcoWardrobe at your city's Earth Day celebration or local sustainability fair. Create a demo booth where visitors can scan clothes and see AI outfit suggestions in real-time. Gather sign-ups and media coverage to build momentum for wider adoption in your community.

Your Journey

From Passion to Purpose

How your environmental passion and volunteer experience leads to innovation

Foundation

"Growing up, I always loved nature and couldn't stand seeing trash in our local parks. I started noticing how much clothing waste ends up in landfills and it bothered me."

Service

"Through Girl Scouts and volunteering at clothing drives, I saw firsthand how donated clothes help families in need. But I also noticed how much 'fast fashion' gets thrown away — barely worn items piling up."

Insight

"I realized the problem isn't that people don't care — it's that they don't know what they already own. They buy new clothes because their closet feels 'boring,' when they actually have plenty of options."

Innovation

"What if AI could help people rediscover their own wardrobe? EcoWardrobe uses technology to make sustainable fashion easy and fun — helping people style what they own and donate what they don't need."
