ONLINE CHILD SAFETY RESEARCH

One cruel post in a class group chat.
Who joins in, who says nothing, and what stops it.

Omniverse simulates the 26 classmates around a pile-on. Each one is an AI agent with its own empathy, need for approval and friendship group, and each decides for itself whether to join in, pass it on, stay quiet, push back, or tell an adult.

Then you try one thing. A classmate speaks up early. A friction prompt before resharing. A message from the form tutor. The run forks, and you compare what the group did in each version.

We do not simulate the child being targeted

The target is a node in the graph with a position and a friendship list. Nothing more. No model is ever asked to write as her, to feel anything, or to react. There is no research question here that needs a language model to perform a 14-year-old's distress, and generating that text would be worse than useless.

The abusive post is not generated either. Agents receive a structured description of it: private photo, mocking caption, 60-person chat, posted by a classmate with high status. That is enough information for an agent to decide what to do, and it means no cruel text about a child exists anywhere in the system.

What we do simulate is the crowd. That is where the interesting question lives anyway, because the crowd is the part an intervention can actually change.

Three steps, about eight minutes

01

Set up

Start from the Redbridge Year 9 class of 26 and edit anyone you want. Pick one intervention to test against doing nothing.

02

Watch

Every decision appears with the reason the agent gave for it. The silences are shown too, which is the part most dashboards leave out.

03

Compare

Two runs side by side, five seeds each. Six numbers, one of which is whether the conversation simply moved somewhere you cannot see.

What is in v0.1, and what we cut

This is a prototype that one engineer can build in a few weeks. The list on the right is short on purpose. Everything we removed was removed because it added build time without changing what you learn from a run.

One class, 26 agents, editable in a table

No graph editor. Friendship groups are a dropdown with three options.

One scenario, three interventions

A classmate defends, a friction prompt before resharing, a message from the form tutor.

Two runs compared, five seeds each

Baseline against one intervention. About $1.40 and eight minutes for the pair.

Cut: the branching timeline tree

Six branches off one run looked good and answered nothing that two runs cannot. It was the most expensive thing in the design.

Cut: accounts, teams, saved experiments

A run is a URL. That is enough to share one with a colleague.

Cut: the written report generator

Five seeds cannot support a written finding. The results page shows the numbers and the spread, and stops there.

Everything Omniverse produces comes from language models playing students who do not exist. It is a way to generate and argue about hypotheses before anyone runs a study with real children. It is not evidence about any real class, and no number from it belongs in a safeguarding decision.
