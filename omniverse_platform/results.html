One classmate speaking up at tick 8 changed the group more than anything else we tried

In the baseline, 14 of 26 students joined in and the thread was still going when the run ended. With a single public push-back eight ticks in, that fell to 6, and four times as many students told an adult. The direction was the same in all five seeds. The size of the gap was not, so read the ordering and ignore the exact numbers.

What the five seeds agree on

Timing mattered more than who did it

We reran the defender at tick 30 instead of tick 8. It made almost no difference to the baseline. By then most students had already replied, and the agents that did so gave reasons about not wanting to contradict themselves. The window is early and it is short.

The silent group got bigger, not smaller

Nine students said nothing in the baseline and eleven said nothing in run B. The push-back moved students out of joining in, but mostly into silence rather than into support. If the goal is for bystanders to act, this intervention does not get you there on its own.

One student decided how far it travelled

Tom Wilde is the only agent in both the year group chat and the football chat. In the four baseline seeds where he forwarded it, the post reached two more chats. In the one where he did not, it stayed in the year group. That is one agent in one graph, so treat it as a question to ask, not an answer.

The result that looks good and is not

An earlier run tested the form tutor posting once in the group chat. Visible posts about her fell 71%, which is the best number on this page. In four of the five seeds the conversation moved into direct messages within three ticks and kept going where nothing counts it.

We would not report that as a win. Any metric built on visible posts will reward an intervention that only hides the behaviour, and that is a failure mode worth designing against before this tool is put in front of a school.

Before you use any of this

  • Five seeds shows direction only. Nothing here supports a percentage claim.
  • These are language models writing what the internet says teenagers do, which is not the same as what teenagers do.
  • The friendship graph is fixed for the whole run. Real fallings-out happen during a pile-on, and we do not model that.
  • The targeted student is a node, so nothing here says anything about the harm done to her.
