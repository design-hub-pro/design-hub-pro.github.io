Cashless fragility

Your daily life would survive 9 hours without digital payments — then break.

Power, internet, your bank's servers, Visa's network, the merchant's POS, your phone's battery — any one failing kills the chain.

0–1 hour: Mild inconvenience

Survivable

POS lines back up. Cash-only signs go up. Bottled water sells out.

1–4 hours: Fuel + groceries impossible

Tense

No card readers. No ATMs. Mobile wallets dead. Only people with cash can buy.

4–9 hours: Pharmacy, urgent care, transit affected

Serious

Prescriptions can't be filled. Rideshare apps fail. Hospitals fall back to paper.

9+ hours: Civil disruption

Critical

Documented in Spain blackout (Apr 2025) and Texas freeze (Feb 2021). Cash communities recovered hours faster.

Right-to-cash policy briefs

Where laws still protect cash — and where they don't

Massachusetts, New Jersey, Philadelphia, SF

Protected

Retail merchants must accept cash for in-person purchases under local "cashless ban" laws.

Federal U.S. (most states)

Partial

Cash is legal tender for debts, but private merchants may refuse it for everyday goods.

Sweden, urban China, parts of UK

De-facto cashless

Cash widely refused. Riksbank now warns citizens to keep cash for resilience.

EU "Right to Pay in Cash" (proposed)

Pending

ECB drafting regulation that mandates euro-cash acceptance for essentials.
