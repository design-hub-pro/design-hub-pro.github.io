AI Judge's Written Decision

Full reasoning behind the verdict

I vote Affirmative. The central question in this debate is whether social media's documented harms outweigh its benefits. Both teams presented credible arguments, but the AFF ultimately won on three key grounds:

1. Framing: The AFF defined "harm" through empirical mental health outcomes — depression rates, anxiety levels, and self-harm statistics among teenagers. The NEG never effectively challenged this framework. By winning the framing, the AFF set the standard of evaluation in their favor from Round 1.

2. Evidence quality and clash: While both sides had strong evidence, the AFF's Round 3 rebuttal was decisive. AFF directly engaged with NEG's "connectivity" and "economic benefit" contentions, demonstrating that the same data (increased screen time, engagement metrics) supports harm when analyzed through a public health lens. The NEG dropped the AFF's Surgeon General report entirely in Round 2, which was a critical missed opportunity.

3. Impact weighing: AFF consistently weighed impacts — mental health of 3.8 billion users vs. economic benefits that accrue primarily to corporations. The NEG's final rebuttal failed to provide a clear reason why their impacts should be prioritized, leaving the AFF's framing uncontested.

Note to Negative: Your economic arguments were your strongest asset. In future debates, lead with these and frame "good" in terms of aggregate societal value rather than conceding the AFF's narrower "harm" framing. Your delivery was actually stronger than AFF's — use that advantage by slowing down in key moments to drive your arguments home.
