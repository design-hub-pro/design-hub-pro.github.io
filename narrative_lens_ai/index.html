The Problem

AI Systems Inherit Historical Power Imbalances

Large language models are trained on vast archives of human-written text—texts that inevitably carry the perspectives, biases, and blind spots of their authors. When users ask AI about immigration, conflict, or social justice, they may unknowingly receive responses shaped by dominant historical narratives while marginalized viewpoints remain invisible.

Growth Path

From Research to Impact

1

Pilot Study

Design 20 experimental prompts across 4 controversial topics. Test on 3 major LLMs (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini). Document initial bias patterns in immigration and conflict narratives.

2

Academic Publication

Expand to 100+ prompts and 6 topic areas. Develop quantitative bias measurement framework. Submit findings to FAccT, CHI, or media studies journals for peer review.

3

Public Engagement

Create interactive web tool for public AI bias testing. Partner with journalism schools and newsrooms. Develop educational curriculum on critical AI literacy.

Your Journey

From Curiosity to Critical Inquiry

How a passion for understanding narratives leads to groundbreaking AI research

Foundation

"I've always been fascinated by how the same historical event can be told so differently depending on who's telling it."

Observation

"When I started using ChatGPT for research, I noticed its answers about colonialism felt... incomplete. Where were the perspectives I learned about in my history classes?"

Insight

"I realized AI doesn't just retrieve information—it inherits the biases of whoever wrote the training data. History is written by victors, and AI is reading their books."

Innovation

"NarrativeLens AI is my way of making invisible biases visible. If we can measure how AI inherits historical narratives, we can work toward more balanced, truthful AI systems."
