The loop

A case arrives as one variant from one family. You pull the real structure, sweep the locator through it until you find the changed residue, then put the typical and variant forms side by side and drag the morph slider until you can see what moved. Only then do you get to write the exhibit.

Writing it is the hard part, and it is the scored part. You assemble explanation cards and hand them to a listener who has never heard the gene name before. If the listener does not follow, the understanding meter stalls and you rebuild the explanation. Being right is not enough. Being understood is the win condition.

That meter is also the study. A research build can record which cards a player chose, how many attempts the explanation took, and whether players who rotated the structure themselves explained it better than players who only read. That is a real comparison, and it is the one question the project is actually set up to answer.

Two things in the brief we would change

The brief says the AI explains "why the mutation may be important". For rare disease that phrasing is risky. Many rare variants are of uncertain significance, and a fluent AI explanation of an uncertain variant is the most dangerous output this project could ship. The game should make uncertainty a visible object: a confidence bar on the case file, and an exhibit that is allowed to open with the plaque reading "we do not know yet". Lighting a room by admitting ignorance should score.

The brief also leans on real structures from scientific databases, which is right, but coverage is the catch. Rare disease genes are exactly the ones least likely to have a solved experimental structure. Expect to fall back to predicted models for most cases, and label which is which on screen. A predicted loop region is not evidence, and the game should not draw it as if it were.

Build detail, scope, and what is not real in these images

Art is concept only. All ten frames are AI-generated concept art for look, layout and mood. They are not captures of a running build. Any text visible inside them is placeholder and should not be read as real data. The images returned square rather than 16:9, so final framing will differ.

Data sources. Variant records from ClinVar, structures from the PDB where an experimental structure exists and AlphaFold DB otherwise, gene and protein function text from UniProt. Every claim in an exhibit should carry the badge of where it came from. No source, no card.

The AI layer. The model's job is rewriting sourced facts at a chosen reading level, not deciding what is true. It should be given the retrieved records and asked to explain them, with a hard rule against asserting pathogenicity that the record does not already state.

Scope for a first build. Eight to twelve cases, each reviewed by someone who knows the gene, is a real deliverable. The floor plan showing hundreds of dark rooms is honest set dressing: it says how much is unknown, and it does not promise to fill them.

Partner. The brief names a collaboration with RareAI. Nothing about that partner's involvement is verified here, and no claim on this page should be read as theirs.
