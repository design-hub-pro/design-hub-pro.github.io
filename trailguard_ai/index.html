The Problem

Our Trails Are Under Invisible Threat

Trail degradation begins long before it becomes visible. By the time we see erosion, widening, or vegetation loss, the damage is often severe and expensive to repair. Current monitoring relies on periodic ranger inspections, which cannot detect early warning signs hidden in how hikers naturally behave on trails.

Growth Path

From Pilot to Platform

1

Local Pilot with Girl Scout Troops

Launch with 5 troops in the Bay Area, partnering with 2-3 local parks. Scouts earn conservation badges while collecting trail health data during regular hiking activities.

2

Regional Expansion & Ranger Integration

Expand to Northern California region with 50+ troops. Integrate with park ranger systems to establish data sharing protocols and validate AI predictions with ground truth.

3

National Platform & Open Citizen Science

Partner with Girl Scouts USA nationally and open the platform to other outdoor organizations. Target 50K+ users contributing data across 500+ parks.

Your Journey

From Passion to Purpose

This project is a natural extension of your environmental values and outdoor leadership experience.

The Narrative Arc: From Scout to Scientist

Foundation: Love for the Outdoors

"My journey began with Girl Scouts, learning to appreciate and respect nature through camping, hiking, and outdoor skills. I saw firsthand how trails connect people to the natural world."

Observation: The Invisible Problem

"On a troop hike, I noticed everyone avoiding the same muddy section—creating a new path through vegetation. That's when I realized: our behavior reveals trail problems before they're visible."

Insight: Data in Every Footstep

"What if we could collect this behavioral data anonymously? Path deviations and walking rhythm changes could be early warning signals—detectable by AI before any ranger inspection."

Innovation: TrailGuard AI

"By combining citizen science with machine learning, I'm turning every Scout hike into a conservation mission. We protect trails not by avoiding them, but by walking them thoughtfully—and letting our footsteps tell the story."
