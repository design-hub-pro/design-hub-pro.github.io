The Problem

The Invisible Connection Between Air and Mind

Research increasingly shows that air pollution affects not just respiratory health but also mental wellbeing. Large epidemiological studies link PM2.5 and NO₂ exposure to increased rates of depression, anxiety, and psychiatric hospital admissions. Yet mental health apps ignore environmental chemical factors in risk assessment and intervention.

Why This Matters for Mental Health

Psychiatric Hospitalizations

Studies across 19 cities show daily PM2.5 and PM10 levels significantly correlate with emergency psychiatric admissions, including for schizophrenia and depression.

Psychiatric Services Review

Cognitive Impairment

A 400ppm CO2 increase causes 21% slower strategic thinking. Indoor environments commonly exceed this, affecting work and learning performance daily.

Harvard T.H. Chan School

Youth Vulnerability

1 in 7 adolescents globally suffer mental disorders. Their developing brains are more sensitive to pollution, with higher inflammatory markers linked to PM2.5 exposure.

PMC Research

Economic Impact

Poor indoor air costs businesses $15 billion annually in lost productivity. Improving ventilation yields $6,500 per person in cognitive performance gains.

Growth Path

From Pilot to Platform

1

Campus Pilot Program

Partner with school counseling services to deploy sensors in study spaces. Collect anonymized data on air-mood correlations among students during exam periods.

2

Research Collaboration

Work with psychology and environmental health researchers to validate air-mental health correlations. Publish findings and refine AI recommendation algorithms.

3

Workplace Wellness

Expand to corporate wellness programs. Offices can use aggregated data to improve ventilation while employees get personal mental health support.
