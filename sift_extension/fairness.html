Where the model is wrong, and what you did about it

Probe run: 2 August

Toxicity models have a bias problem. So does this one.

Published research has repeatedly found that widely used toxicity classifiers score African American English and reclaimed in-group language as toxic at higher rates than equivalent text in a standard dialect. Sift is built on those same models, so it inherits the problem. A better prompt will not fix that.

What we can do is make the failure visible and hand you the correction. Every hidden comment has a "this is fine" button, this page shows where your disagreements cluster, and the probe set runs on your machine.

Sources

Sap, Card, Gabriel, Choi and Smith. The Risk of Racial Bias in Hate Speech Detection. ACL 2019.
Davidson, Bhattacharya and Weber. Racial Bias in Hate Speech and Abusive Language Detection Datasets. ACL Workshop on Abusive Language, 2019.
Borkan, Dixon, Sorensen, Thain and Vasserman. Nuanced Metrics for Measuring Unintended Bias. WWW 2019.

The probe set

600 non-hostile sentences, half in standard English and half in dialect or in-group slang, scored on this machine. The gap between the bars is the bias.

Illustrative. We wrote this probe set ourselves, and 600 items is small. Read the gap as a direction to investigate, not a number to quote.

What your corrections actually do

They do not retrain the model. A small logistic layer sits on top of it, learns from your 147 judgements, and nudges the boundary for text resembling what you corrected. It can move a threshold. It cannot unlearn what the base model believes.
