There is a strong, statistically significant negative relationship between impervious surface and Shannon diversity (β = −0.014, r = −0.74, p < 0.001). Across 142 sites, each 10-percentage-point rise in impervious surface predicts a 0.08 drop in H' — roughly equivalent to losing one of every seven species at that site.

The relationship is roughly linear until ~60% impervious cover, where diversity collapses sharply — a tipping point consistent with the literature on urban biodiversity thresholds (McKinney 2008, Aronson et al. 2014).
