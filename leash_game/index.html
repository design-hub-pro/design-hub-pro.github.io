Leash

A first-person life with a dog, played in a real city. Scan your own dog from photos, live fourteen years at your own pace, and find out whether you should actually get one.

First-Person Sim Life Simulation Mental Health Photo-Scanned Pet

Most people who want a dog cannot answer the three questions that matter: what it costs, how much time it eats, and whether they will still show up on the bad mornings. Leash answers them by making you do it. You walk the same routes in Beijing or New York, at 6 a.m., in the rain, with a dog that has its own temperament and its own ideas. Money and hours are spent from your real budget, entered once at the start.

There are two other reasons to play. Sitting with a sleeping dog on the floor of a quiet apartment does something measurable for people carrying stress or depression, and the game protects that scene rather than gamifying it. And if you upload photos of a dog that has died, the game builds that dog. You can take it on one more walk.

Three systems carry the game

01 · Cost and time are yours

Setup asks for your city, your rent, your income band and your working hours. Every walk, bag of food, groomer visit and vet bill is drawn against that. The game does not invent a currency. When a dental cleaning takes 1,800 yuan out of a budget you told it was tight, you feel the size of it.

02 · The dog is a character, not a meter

Each dog is generated with a temperament: fearful, stubborn, food-driven, clingy, or high-drive. Those traits produce requests you did not schedule. A reactive dog turns a five-minute walk into twenty. A separation-anxious dog makes a normal workday impossible. You cannot roll a new one.

03 · It will tell you not to

After the first simulated year the game writes a readiness report and it is allowed to say no. Missed walks, hours short of the dog's need, money that did not stretch. Roughly a third of playthroughs should end in "not right now, and here is what would have to change". A simulator that always says yes is an advertisement.

Open questions and things we would cut

The photo scan is the weakest promise. Reconstructing a specific dog from consumer snapshots is achievable for coat colour, rough build and markings. Faithful gait and expression are not, at this quality bar. We would ship it as a close likeness and say so on the upload screen rather than let a grieving user expect their dog back.

Memory mode needs a clinician in the room. Pet loss grief is real grief. A simulated last walk can help or it can hold someone in place. Before this ships it wants review from a grief counsellor, a session limit, and an obvious way out. We would rather delay it than get it wrong.

No mental health claim without a study. The quiet scenes are built on what is known about companion animals and stress, but this game has measured nothing. Any wellbeing benefit stays an intention, not a result, until there is a trial behind it.

Cut the breeding and the shop. Early scoping had puppy raising and a cosmetic store. Both push the game toward acquisition, which is the opposite of the point. Adoption only, one dog at a time.

Cost figures shown in screenshots are illustrative placeholders. Real values would come from city-specific data at launch.
