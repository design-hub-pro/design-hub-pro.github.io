Research Proposal

Food Chemistry as the Core, Data Science as the Method

This project asks a genuine food-chemistry question — which ingredient substitutions preserve the chemistry that makes food taste good while removing what harms older adults — then answers it with small, controlled kitchen experiments and quantitative data analysis. The app is simply how the findings reach real families. AI is optional and stays in a supporting role.

Central Research Question

"When we substitute an ingredient to cut sugar, sodium, or saturated fat, which underlying chemical reactions must be preserved so the dish still browns, binds, and tastes right — and can we prove it in a real kitchen?"

C₆

RQ1 · Maillard & browning

Which low-glycemic sweeteners still carry the carbonyl group needed for the Maillard reaction, so baked goods brown and taste caramelized?

Na⁺

RQ2 · Umami vs. sodium

Can glutamate- and nucleotide-rich foods replace up to half the NaCl in savory dishes without a detectable loss in perceived saltiness?

RQ3 · Fat & emulsion

Which mono-unsaturated fats or protein/starch gels keep an emulsion stable when saturated fat is removed from sauces and baked goods?

Six Concrete Deliverables

Exactly the outputs the family asked for — scoped so the chemistry stays central and the workload is realistic for one student across ~9 months.

What a Database Rule Looks Like

Two worked examples — the chemical mechanism is what makes each rule a research finding rather than a cooking tip.

SucroseAllulose

Mechanism: allulose keeps a free carbonyl group, so it still browns via the Maillard reaction — but is barely metabolized (~0.4 kcal/g, near-zero glycemic response). Beats erythritol, which won't brown.

NaClKCl + shiitake

Mechanism: potassium chloride replaces ~50% of sodium; glutamate and 5′-nucleotides from dried mushroom trigger umami synergy that masks KCl's metallic bitterness.

Experiment Protocol

  • Cook a control and a modified batch under identical conditions (same oven, time, pan).
  • Compute nutrition deltas from USDA FoodData Central; estimate glycemic load from the Sydney GI database.
  • Record browning (photo colorimetry, ΔE), rise, and texture against the predicted chemistry.
  • Run a blind taste panel on the 9-point hedonic scale (Peryam & Pilgrim, 1957).

Social Impact

Built for older adults living with diet-sensitive conditions

Over 1 in 3 adults 65+ live with diabetes or hypertension, where everyday cooking is a daily health decision. NutriChem Lab turns peer-reviewed food chemistry into simple, familiar-recipe swaps — and shares weekly reports with the family members and caregivers who cook for them. The research is validated with senior taste panels, so the science is grounded in the people it serves.
