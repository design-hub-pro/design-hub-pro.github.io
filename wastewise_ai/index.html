The Problem

The Gap Between Policy and Practice

Governments worldwide have implemented mandatory waste classification policies, but compliance is often superficial. Residents may place items in the correct bins without genuinely sorting—mixing recyclables with contaminants, or disposing waste at wrong times. Current systems rely on visual inspections and self-reporting, which cannot distinguish between "formal compliance" and "genuine sorting behavior."

Growth Path

From 1 to 100

1

Run a Pilot at Your School Cafeteria

Talk to your school's sustainability coordinator or principal about installing 2-3 sensor-equipped bins in the cafeteria. Schools often welcome student-led environmental initiatives, and cafeterias generate consistent, measurable waste data daily.

2

Partner with a Local Apartment Complex

Contact property managers at nearby apartment communities—they often deal with recycling contamination fines and would welcome a solution. Offer a free 2-month pilot in exchange for access to their waste area and resident feedback.

3

Present to Your Local HOA or Community Board

Many residential communities struggle with recycling contamination and would welcome a data-driven solution. Bring your school pilot results to a community meeting—this is how real startups find their first customers.
